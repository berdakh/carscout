The 10-step playbook

  1. Set the real budget. The payment is half the story. Add insurance, fuel, and about $40 to $70 a month for repairs. Use tab 1.
  2. Pick two or three models, not one. Boring and common is good: parts are cheap and mechanics know them.
  3. Shop every channel. Dealer sites and Autotrader for selection, Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist for price, Carvana and CarMax for a 7-day return. Tab 2 shows the typical price gap.
  4. Get the VIN before you drive anywhere. Run a free NHTSA recall check and spend $25 to $45 on a Carfax or AutoCheck report. A seller who will not share the VIN is done.
  5. Grade the listing. Compare to market, read the red flags, decide your walk-away price before you go.
  6. Inspect and test drive (checklist below). Cold start only. Bring a friend.
  7. Pay for a pre-purchase inspection at an independent shop, $100 to $200. It pays for itself the first time it finds a $1,500 problem.
  8. Negotiate from facts. Start at the suggested offer, name the issues the inspection found, and be ready to leave.
  9. Arrange financing first. Get pre-approved at a credit union, then let the dealer try to beat it. Never shop by monthly payment alone; ask the total price.
  10. Paperwork. Title in the seller's name, matching VIN, odometer statement, bill of sale, lien release if there was a loan. Meet at a bank or DMV for private sales.

Inspection checklist

  • Cold start: no rattles, smoke, or warning lights.
  • Oil: level and color. Milky means coolant mixing, walk away.
  • Coolant: not rusty or oily.
  • Tires: even wear, same brand, date code under 6 years.
  • Brakes: no pulsing or pulling, listen for grinding.
  • Transmission: smooth shifts, no slipping or shuddering, test reverse.
  • Steering: no wandering, clunks over bumps, or vibration at 60 mph.
  • Body: panel gaps, paint texture differences, overspray on trim, mismatched bolts under the hood mean collision repair.
  • Frame rails and rocker panels: rust, fresh undercoating hides it.
  • Water: musty smell, damp carpet, silt under the seats or in the trunk mean flood.
  • Electronics: every window, lock, light, AC on max, heat, infotainment, cameras.
  • Check engine light: if it is off but the dash does not show it during the key-on bulb check, it has been disabled.

Red flags and scams

  • Price far below market with a story (military deployment, divorce, moving abroad).
  • Asks for a deposit before you see the car, or payment by gift card, wire, or crypto.
  • "Title coming" or title in another person's name. Curbstoners flip cars without registering them.
  • No test drive, no inspection, "sold as-is, no exceptions" from a private seller.
  • Odometer lower than the history report, or mileage that does not match the pedal and wheel wear.
  • Fresh undercoating, new carpet in an old car, or a single repainted panel.
  • Seller will not meet at a bank, police station, or DMV.
